# Results log

A month-by-month narrative of what was found, in the order the work was done. Every number comes from the printed reports in `results/reports/`. No session dates or times appear here. Glucose in mmol/L throughout. Analysis only; nothing here informs a dosing decision.

## September 2026, first week: the 5-minute forecaster

**Discovery and alignment.** The four exports were parsed, cross-checked and aligned on a 5-minute grid (see `docs/data_sources.md`). The train/test split was fixed before any model was scored: the last three weeks of the second data window are the test set, everything earlier is training. The split has not moved since.

**Baselines first.** On the clean test rows the last-value baseline (glucose in 30 minutes = glucose now) had RMSE 1.534 mmol/L and a straight-line trend fitted to the last hour was worse (2.259). A LightGBM point model on past-only features reached 1.207 on the same rows, scored once. Post-exercise rows were the hardest regime for every model.

**The band.** Quantile models gave an 80% interval that under-covered where it mattered (about half of the readings below 4 fell inside it on validation). A conformal-style widening by risk group, chosen on validation only, was applied before the single test run. The verdict (`forecaster_verdict.txt`): coverage 84.1% overall, 65.2% below 4 mmol/L, 60.6% within four hours of exercise. The band is therefore declared as an 80% interval that is known to under-cover during lows; the alert, not the band, covers lows.

**The low alert.** A dedicated classifier for "any reading below 4 in the next 30 minutes" with isotonic calibration and a 0.60 operating threshold caught all 24 low episodes of the test period at 2.2 alert episodes a day (Brier 0.0228 against a constant-prevalence 0.0449). The alert-lead analysis added later (`alert_lead_report.txt`) shows how short that warning usually was: median 5 minutes before the first low, only 2 of 24 episodes with 20 minutes or more.

**Does exercise information help the forecaster?** Ablations on validation and in rolling-origin cross-validation showed small gains from exercise and movement features concentrated in the hours after a workout, not overall. That question was then moved to the session level, where it can be asked directly.

## September 2026, third week: sessions, controls and the first models

**Prompt 01, the session table.** One row per exercise session (gym, cricket, football) from the watch's workouts, with glucose, insulin, food and pump state at the start and low outcomes during the session and in the two hours after. Sessions include a low; they do not necessarily end because of one.

**Prompt 02, matched controls.** The author has lows on most days, so a session's low rate means nothing on its own. Each usable session was matched to up to three exercise-free windows at the same clock time on nearby days. On the 89 usable sessions of the two original windows: 43.8% included a low against 39.7% in the matched rest windows, ratio 1.10 (bootstrap 95% interval 0.82 to 1.43). Football stood out (ratio 3.75, 1.80 to 10.50, on eight sessions); gym did not (0.93). Lows were most common when the session started two to four hours after a meal (64%), and that band was also the riskiest without exercise (47% in the controls). `sessions_v2_controls_report.txt`.

**Prompt 03, small logistic models.** Pooled sessions and controls, weighted so that each session and its controls count equally, forward-chained by calendar month, compared with a paired cluster bootstrap. Starting glucose and its 30-minute slope (B1) beat the base rate; adding the 4-hour insulin total beat adding the pump's own 2-hour insulin-on-board pair; adding exercise helped further but its Brier improvement over B1 did not clear zero. Fitted once on all rows, exercise raised the odds of a low about 1.9-fold after glucose, slope and insulin. The exercise-by-insulin interaction was the clearest term: each extra unit of insulin in the previous four hours multiplied the exercise effect by about 1.33.

**Prompt 04, the December back-fill.** A fourth data window was added at the front (the first month the watch was worn), pulled through the same exports and the browser session. Rows from the two original windows stayed byte-identical after the rebuild.

**Prompt 05, tidy-up, re-run and pump lead time.** Watch "Other" fragments adjacent to gym workouts were folded into gym; three December CGM gaps were flagged. Re-run with the December sessions as training-only fold: exercise odds ratio 2.12 (1.25 to 3.60), interaction 1.33 (1.15 to 1.61), 4-hour total still ahead of the 2-hour pair (Brier difference -0.009, interval -0.021 to +0.002). `pooled_models_report_prompt05_109sessions.txt`. The lead-time analysis (`lead_time_report.txt`) then asked, for the 44 sessions that included a low, when the body, the pump and the glucose each first signalled: steps rose a median 20 minutes before the start, heart rate at the start, the pump first cut delivery a median 27.5 minutes after the start, and the first low came a median 97.5 minutes after the start. Movement led the pump's first cut by a median 50 minutes (heart rate: 25 minutes); in 9 of 42 sessions the low came before the pump's first cut.

**Prompt 06, last labels and the anticipation test.** Four more "Other" entries became gym by dated override, one entry became a cycling session (unusable because a CGM gap sits inside it), and sessions shorter than 15 minutes on the grid were marked unusable. The usable set became 110 sessions (gym 70, indoor cricket 15, outdoor cricket 15, football 10). The anticipation test (`anticipation_report.txt`) asked how far ahead a system could have known a session was coming without being told: a routine rule (same weekday and half-hour slot in two of the previous four weeks) flagged 12% of sessions an hour ahead at 1.3 false alarms a week, and is essentially a detector of the two fixed weekly fixtures; a movement rule (steps or heart rate) flagged 96% but only 25 to 40 minutes ahead and at about 50 false-alarm episodes a week, because it fires on any walk.

**Prompt 07, the insulin curve.** Replacing the pump's 2-hour straight-line insulin-on-board with a 5-hour exponential curve (and adding carbs-on-board and exercise interactions) changed the low classifier's cross-validated Brier score by +0.0001 (interval -0.0004 to +0.0006): nothing, as predicted and logged before the run. The only regime where a difference cleared zero was the four hours after exercise (about a 2 to 3% relative improvement), one of 24 cells, kept as a lead for a pre-registered check rather than a result. `cv_classifier_v2_report.txt`.

**Prompt 08, this repository.** The session models were re-run on the published tables (110 sessions, identifiers replaced by random ids, no dates): `pooled_models_report.txt`. The point estimates match a private re-run on the original tables exactly; the bootstrap intervals differ in the last digits because the set order changed with the identifiers. Against the 109-session run of prompt 05 the numbers moved for two reasons, both inside the December training-only fold: the one admitted 15-minute session and its three controls were added, and the three short December gym entries marked unusable in prompt 06 now count as exercise for the control-window exclusion, so several December control windows moved to other days. The January fold's predictions come from a model trained on that December fold alone, so the out-of-fold Brier scores shifted (B1 0.228 to 0.242) and so did the odds ratios (exercise 2.12 to 2.32); the four-hour-total against two-hour-pair difference, which the earlier run put at -0.009 with the interval touching zero, is +0.001 (-0.010 to +0.013) in the re-run. The interaction term did not move (1.33).

## Open points carried forward

- The football effect rests on ten sessions.
- Controls are matched on clock time and nearness in date, not on what the author did (Activity mode, carbohydrate before exercise), so the exercise effect is the effect of an exercise session as actually managed.
- The 30-minute alert has no counterfactual: nothing here shows that acting on it earlier would have prevented a low.
